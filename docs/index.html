Xanomeline for Alzheimer's disease: safety reanalysis of the CDISC pilot trial

A 26-week, double-blind, placebo-controlled study of a xanomeline skin patch at two doses. I took the raw trial data (SDTM) to analysis datasets (ADaM), built the safety tables and figures, ran a time-to-event analysis, queried the same data in SQL, and checked my own derivations with an independent second program.

    How it was built

    What this shows

    • CDISC standards. SDTM domains in, ADaM datasets (ADSL, ADAE, ADVS, ADLB, ADTTE) out, with written specifications.
    • Pharmaverse. Derivations with admiral: date imputation, merged variables, population flags.
    • Safety TLFs. Demographics, disposition, adverse events by body system and preferred term, vital signs, lab shift tables.
    • Survival analysis. Kaplan–Meier, log-rank test and a Cox model.
    • Double programming. An independent base-R version, compared with diffdf.
    • SQL. DuckDB queries, including joins and window functions.
    • Testing. Unit tests for every derivation helper with testthat.
    • Reproducible. One script, run_all.R, rebuilds everything from the public data.

    Data and limits

    The data are the CDISC SDTM/ADaM Pilot Project study (CDISCPILOT01), released publicly for training, and distributed in R by the pharmaversesdtm package. No personal data are involved.

    This is a training reanalysis, not the sponsor's analysis. The efficacy endpoints (ADAS-Cog, CIBIC+) are not covered. Some derivation choices (treatment-emergent window, "worst" lab category, the dermatologic event definition) are mine and are written down in the specs.
